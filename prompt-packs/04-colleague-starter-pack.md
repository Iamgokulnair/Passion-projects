# 04 - Colleague starter pack

For people starting with an AI assistant at work (Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT). No code. Use it
as a job aid in a first session, then leave it with the team.

Replace anything in `[brackets]` before you paste.

---

## The one habit that matters

Every good prompt says four things: **who you are, what you need, what good looks like,
and what it must not do.** Most weak output comes from leaving out the last two.

---

## Prompts by role

### Anyone - first week

```
I'm a [role] and I spend about [X] hours a week on [task]. Ask me five questions about how
I do it today, then suggest two ways you could take part of it off my plate. Be specific
about which steps you'd do and which I should keep.
```

```
Here is a document I wrote: [paste]. Tell me the three places a busy senior reader would
stop reading, and why. Don't rewrite it yet.
```

### Team leads and managers

```
Here are this month's team numbers: [paste]. Tell me what changed against last month,
what's worth raising in a review, and what's noise. Show the arithmetic for every change
you call out. If a number looks wrong, say so rather than explaining it.
```

```
I'm preparing a check-in with a colleague. Here are their notes and my observations:
[paste, with names removed]. Draft three open questions I could ask. Don't write
feedback for me.
```

### Delivery and operations

```
Here is an incoming request: [paste]. Before anyone starts work, list what is unclear or
missing. Group the gaps by what would cause rework if we guessed wrong.
```

```
Turn these meeting notes into decisions, owners and dates: [paste]. Only list an owner or
date if it's actually stated. Mark anything else as "not agreed".
```

### Learning and enablement

```
I'm running a 45-minute session for [audience] on [topic]. Their current level is
[describe]. Give me an agenda with one hands-on exercise that uses their real work, and
one question to check they got it.
```

## Safe-use guide (one page)

**Do**
- Remove names, client details and anything confidential before pasting, unless your
  firm's approved tool is cleared for that data.
- Use only the AI tools your organisation has approved for work data.
- Check every number, name, date and quote against the source. AI output is a first draft.
- Say when you used AI, if your team or client expects to know.

**Don't**
- Paste passwords, personal data or unreleased financials.
- Send AI output to a client or senior reader without reading every line.
- Ask it for facts you can't check. If you can't verify it, don't use it.

**When it gets something wrong:** tell it exactly what was wrong and ask again. Starting a
new chat loses what it learned in this one.

---

## Job aid: is this output ready to send?

- [ ] I could explain every claim in it without the AI.
- [ ] Every number traces back to a source I've seen.
- [ ] No confidential detail went into a tool that isn't approved for it.
- [ ] It sounds like me, not like a chatbot.
- [ ] The reader knows what I need from them.

---

## Measuring whether it's working

Track three things per team, weekly: how many people used the tool, how many named one
task that got faster, and one real example of changed work. The example matters more than
the count - it's what convinces the next person.
