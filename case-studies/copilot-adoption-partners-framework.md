# Adoption Partners: a framework for rolling out Copilot to internal teams

A self-authored, open case study. The company is illustrative: a global tech company with
10,000+ employees across three regions, midway through a Microsoft 365 Copilot rollout.
Licences are live and usage is flat. Nobody is short of training material. The question is
why people who were keen in week one stopped by week six.

I wrote this in my own time, as practice in thinking through colleague enablement end to
end. Nothing here is drawn from an employer's data.

---

## The diagnosis

A first "AI Champions" pilot launched with an all-hands webinar and a welcome pack. Within
six weeks most champions had stopped taking part. Exit feedback named two causes, and
neither was the material:

1. **No clear remit.** Champions didn't know what they'd signed up for, or how much time it was.
2. **No reinforcement.** They raised questions and nobody answered them.

A better welcome pack wouldn't have saved it. The fix is a written contract and a
guaranteed answer.

---

## The framework: four moves, mapped to ADKAR

| Move | What happens | ADKAR stage |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Define the mandate | A one-page charter per partner: the opportunities in their own workflows, the time it takes (two hours a month), the recognition they earn, a clean way to step back. Their line manager countersigns it. | Awareness, Desire |
| 02 Let evidence recruit | Each partner rebuilds one task from their own week with Copilot and files it on a one-page template: the task, time before, time after, what they'd tell a colleague. | Knowledge, Ability |
| 03 Reward in public | Every use case goes into a shared library under the partner's name, visible to their own leadership. | Ability, Reinforcement |
| 04 Instrument, then hand over | Measure at week 8, and name the owner who carries it into business as usual. | Reinforcement |

---

## Eight weeks, cohort by cohort

| Weeks | Phase | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Define | Charters agreed with partner and manager; each partner's reason for saying yes captured in 15 minutes |
| 2-3 | Prove | First use case filed per partner; cross-team trios formed; one 45-minute session per region, recorded once |
| 4-5 | Amplify | Library live; central team answers every partner question within 5 working days and reports its own record |
| 6-7 | Scale | Partners apply a colleague's use case to their own workflow; a second pulse survey |
| 8 | Council | One-page brief to the steering group: three numbers, one story, one ask |

Cohort 1 is 40 partners across three regions. Each later cohort starts with Cohort 1's
library, so it starts ahead.

---

## The loop that runs it

**Ship -> publish -> respond.** Once a month a partner files one real use case. Any question
they raise gets a written answer inside five working days. The use case and the answer both
go to the whole cohort, not just the person who asked. The library becomes the earliest
read on adoption, months before usage dashboards would show it.

The five-day answer commitment is the part I'd protect first if budget moved. It costs the
least and it's the thing the failed pilot didn't have.

---

## Measuring it honestly

Leadership's target is "80% of partners feel equipped". That's a sentiment measure, so every
number is reported with the population it was measured across:

- how many of the 40 answered the pulse
- how many published a use case
- how many questions were answered inside five working days

A high score from 12 respondents out of 40 is reported as exactly that.

---

## What I'd need from partner functions

| Function | Ask | Indicative time |
|---|---|---|
| HR | A nomination brief to leaders; the charter counted in the performance conversation | ~4 hrs in weeks 1-3, then ~1 hr a month |
| L&D | A home for the use-case library; one facilitator for the regional sessions | ~6 hrs in weeks 2-5 |
| Internal Comms | One publication slot at week 6 for partner-written results | ~2 hrs a month from week 3 |

These are proposals. I'd spend 30 minutes with each function in week one to agree the size
before promising anything to a business unit.

---

## What I'd ask for first

The nomination record: what each partner was told, why they said yes, and what their
manager agreed to. It decides whether week one is onboarding or re-contracting. Usage
telemetry is useful, but access to it varies by region and policy, so I treat it as a
week-one feasibility check, not the foundation.

---

## Transferable to any internal AI rollout

Swap Copilot for any assistant. The mechanics hold: a written contract, one real use case
per person, a public library, a guaranteed answer, and numbers reported with their
population.
